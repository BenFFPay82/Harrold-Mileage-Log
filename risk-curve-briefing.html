Risk Curve Visualiser

How to use it — a quick guide for the Community Risk Analysis group

Proof of concept

This is an interactive tool that visualises the risk-curve idea for the Community Risk Analysis, tested on dwelling fires. Instead of treating a dwelling fire as one average risk, it spreads the full range of outcomes — from a pan fire that is out on arrival, to a fire that takes multiple lives — across a likelihood × consequence curve, so the rare-but-serious events stay visible. Use it to explore how different scoring choices change the picture before we agree a method for the next CRMP.

Opening it

  1. Double-click risk-curve.html — it opens in any web browser (Edge, Chrome).
  2. Nothing to install. It is one self-contained file; you can email it or drop it on SharePoint.
  3. To save a copy as PDF, press Ctrl + P → Save as PDF. It prints cleanly on A4.

The controls (top of the page)

  • Matrix format — switch between a 5 × 5 and 7 × 7 grid to test whether the format changes the answer.
  • Lens shown — view risk through one lens (People, Place, Environment, Economy) or the Combined view.
  • Combine method — how the four lenses become one score (weighted average, weighted impact, or worst-lens).
  • Weight sliders — set your risk appetite, e.g. weight People above property. They adjust everything live.
  • Show Ian’s single-point method — overlays the older 2022 approach for comparison (see opposite).

Editing the data

  • Every likelihood and consequence value in the data table is a drop-down — change one and the matrix, curve and scores update instantly.
  • The figures shipped with the tool are illustrative placeholders; real CRA-database numbers get plugged in here.

Reading the screen

  • Risk matrix — likelihood up the side, consequence along the bottom. Cell colour is the risk band: tolerable, tolerable with mitigation, unacceptable. Numbered dots are the dwelling-fire outcomes.
  • Risk curve — the same outcomes plotted as a curve, from frequent/low-consequence (top-left) to rare/high-consequence (bottom-right). Hover any point for detail.
  • Headline figures — quick read-outs for the current settings (peak score, size of the high-consequence tail, most-weighted lens).
  • Compare & contrast table — ranks every outcome under Ian’s method vs the new 5 × 5 vs 7 × 7, as a % of each scale’s maximum.

Why it differs from the 2022 method

  • The 2022 assessment scored each incident type as a single averaged point — averaging hides the rare fatal fires.
  • This tool keeps the whole spread visible and lets the matrix lean on consequence, so a rare catastrophe outscores a frequent triviality.
  • Tick the overlay (blue diamond) to see the single averaged point sit low on the curve.

Please remember

This is a concept visualiser, not a final methodology. The data is illustrative and several choices — likelihood proportions, the combine method, the 7-level definitions and appetite thresholds — are still for the group to agree. Use it to inform that discussion, not to draw conclusions yet.
